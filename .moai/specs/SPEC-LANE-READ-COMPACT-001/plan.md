- Iter2 arithmetic (plan-audit iter2 repair; the cut reserved in the table). Each row renders `{"id":"…16…","state":"picked","prefix":"…243…"}`: 7 + 16 + 11 + 6 + 12 + 243 + 2 = 297 bytes at the escapable worst case, plus one comma between rows, over a 224-byte nine-key envelope that carries no identifier, so cap n = 224 + 297·n + (n − 1). Cap 6 = 224 + 1,782 + 5 = **2,011** (headroom 37 bytes); cap 7 = 224 + 2,079 + 6 = 2,309. The 0.3.0 table (id at 8 bytes, rows of 289 bytes) is superseded: the cut consumes 8 bytes per row that it did not reserve, and the figures above are recomputed with it (arithmetic checked against the recorded L-12 figures at the pre-cut widths: 1,673 / 1,963 / 2,253 / 2,543 / 3,123 all reproduce exactly, so the envelope and per-row model are the script's own).
- Measured on the live queue at the observation (163 live items, at the L-12 run): cap 6 gives 817 bytes, cap 10 gives 1,273, cap 15 gives 1,833, and cap 20 gives 2,355 (over the bound on that queue).
- Correction to the working hypothesis. The figure of about 1,755 bytes at cap 10 assumed 3-byte CJK prefixes with no JSON escapes. The prefix function does not escape `<`, `>`, `&`, control characters, U+2028, or U+2029, and the JSON encoder writes each of them as a 6-byte escape. Counting those runes at their JSON width, cap 10 is 3,203 bytes and fails (the 0.3.0 text carried 3,123 at the pre-cut id width; the cut adds 8 bytes per row). For CJK-only text, cap 10 is 2,003 bytes and cap 11 is 2,181 bytes (fail) — a pass at cap 10 that does not move the choice, because the escapable worst case above, not the CJK-only case, is the binding one: cap 7 already fails there. The bound must hold for any queue content, and it does: the bounded form cuts each row's `id` to the declared 16-byte encoded width (§ C.1, the plan-audit iter2 repair), so no store state escapes the table; the cap is 6.
- Operator option, not chosen: a prefix bounded by encoded width (the first 40 JSON-encoded bytes, then `...`) would allow cap 18, at a worst case of 1,987 bytes (cap 19 gives 2,085 and fails). This changes the prefix rule for the compact form only. (Recomputed at the iter2 id cut; the 0.3.0 text allowed cap 20 at 2,023 bytes at the pre-cut id width.)
