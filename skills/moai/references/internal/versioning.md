# Internal: versioning

When to read: before you ask the `versioning` rows of the question bank (`internal/interview`) in the PROJECT route, and before you compute or check a release version, which `## At every release` below does at every release.

MoAI offers three schemes: SemVer is the default, HeadVer the second, CalVer the third (when the work flow is m-flow, HeadVer is labelled "suggested with m-flow" and SemVer stays first and recommended). Anything else is the user's own words, recorded as `custom`. The choice is the profile key `versioning`: `scheme` is `semver`, `headver`, `calver` or `custom`; `format` is the CalVer format, or for `custom` the user's words in one line (at most 128 characters; no control character, no `<`, `>`, backtick, `[`, `]`, `*`, `_`, `/` or `\`, no `#` at the start, no line or paragraph separator (U+2028, U+2029), and no character Unicode marks as default-ignorable, which shows nothing: the Default_Ignorable_Code_Point set of Unicode 18.0.0, U+00AD, U+034F, U+061C, U+115F–U+1160, U+17B4–U+17B5, U+180B–U+180F, U+200B–U+200F, U+202A–U+202E, U+2060–U+206F, U+3164, U+FE00–U+FE0F, U+FEFF, U+FFA0, U+FFF0–U+FFF8, U+1BCA0–U+1BCA3, U+1D173–U+1D17A, U+E0000–U+E0FFF; no format character, which hides or changes how the text around it is drawn: General_Category Cf of Unicode 18.0.0, U+00AD, U+0600–U+0605, U+061C, U+06DD, U+070F, U+0890–U+0891, U+08E2, U+180E, U+200B–U+200F, U+202A–U+202E, U+2060–U+2064, U+2066–U+206F, U+FEFF, U+FFF9–U+FFFB, U+110BD, U+110CD, U+13430–U+1343F, U+1BCA0–U+1BCA3, U+1D173–U+1D17A, U+E0001, U+E0020–U+E007F; and no credential shape: a key prefix at the start of a word or a token or password assignment is refused, while a prefix inside a word, as in `task-based` or `risk-free`, is ordinary text); `head` is the HeadVer head number, at most 9999. `project-plan` refuses any other scheme, a `format` on SemVer or HeadVer, a `head` on anything but HeadVer, and a `versioning` that is not an object. A profile without the key has no recorded choice: ask, never assume SemVer. The `AGENTS.md` managed section carries the choice as one line, `Versioning: …`.

## Keep the existing scheme

`moai project-detect` reports `versioning` beside `detected`, read only: `scheme` (`semver`, `calver`, `headver`, `mixed` or `none`), `latest` (the highest version it recognised, as written), `evidence` (the five highest tags, then the version in the root `Cargo.toml`, `package.json` and `pyproject.toml`, each with the `shape` it was read as) and `tags` (`read`, `no_repository`, or `unavailable` with a `tags_reason`). "Highest" is version order, not date: numbers compared segment by segment, then the pre-release in SemVer 2.0.0 order (`rc.10` above `rc.2`, a release above its pre-releases), so a name like `v1.9.9` tagged after `v2.0.0` does not become `latest`. With `mixed`, `evidence` also holds the highest tag of each scheme it counted that is not among the five. Tag names are read from the files of the project's own `.git` (`refs/tags` and `packed-refs`; a linked worktree's pointer is followed to the repository that names this folder back as one of its worktrees, nothing else is); no program is started.

1. When `scheme` names one scheme, keeping it is the first and recommended option, shown with that scheme and `latest` so the user can correct a wrong reading. Never recommend a change the user did not ask for: published versions, and every tool that sorts them, depend on the scheme.
2. Tags decide. The manifests decide only when no tag has a shape.
3. Shapes overlap, and the evidence rows say where. A row with `shape` `ambiguous` (`26.4.1` is valid SemVer and a possible CalVer; `16.4` is a possible CalVer or a plain two-part version) is not counted: with `none` and such a row, show the row in the question; there is no keep option to offer. Every HeadVer version is also valid SemVer, and its row carries the note "also valid SemVer". A HeadVer shape counts as SemVer only beside a SemVer tag with the same first number (a minor that walks through `1.999.0`, `1.1000.0`, `1.1001.0`). When the HeadVer heads left are all above every SemVer major, every HeadVer tag sorts above every SemVer one: that is a move to HeadVer, reported as `headver` (`v3.0.0`, `v3.2.0`, then `4.2647.1`, `4.2647.3` read `headver` with `latest` `4.2647.3`, and keeping it keeps head 4). Any other mix of the two is `mixed` (`v5.0.0` beside `3.2647.1`). HeadVer shapes alone are reported as `headver`, which a SemVer project whose minor happens to look like a year and week would also show. The user's pick decides, not the detection.
4. `mixed` means the tags show two schemes: say which, then ask without a keep option.
5. `tags` `unavailable` or `no_repository` means no tag was read: `none` then says nothing about the project's history; say so when you ask, with the `tags_reason`.
6. A change of scheme must still sort above every published version. SemVer to HeadVer: `head` is the old MAJOR plus one. SemVer to CalVer: the year segment must be greater than the old MAJOR. From CalVer with a full year to SemVer or HeadVer no smaller first number sorts above the old versions: say so before the user picks.

How `project-detect` reads one version, after dropping a leading `v` and a prefix that ends in `/` or `@`, and ignoring what follows `-` or `+`:

| Shape | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `calver` | Two to four numeric segments; the first is a four-digit year from 2000, or a two-digit number from 10 followed by a month from 1 to 12 (with only two segments the month must be zero-padded: `26.04`) |
| `headver` | Three numeric segments; the middle one has four digits, a two-digit year from 10 and a week from 01 to 53 |
| `semver` | Three numeric segments without leading zeros that fit neither rule above |
| `ambiguous` | Fits two readings: a two-digit year and a month that is also valid SemVer (`26.4.1`), two segments without a padded month (`16.4`, `16.10`), or a HeadVer middle segment behind a four-digit year |
| `unrecognised` | Anything else |

## At every release

Run this list yourself, without being asked, whenever you prepare, tag or publish a release or a release candidate. The list itself runs autonomously; pushing a tag or publishing a release cannot be undone, so it is a question that waits for the user's pick at every autonomy level, `full` included, with no deadline default.

1. Scheme: read the profile's `versioning`. Without the profile key, run the `versioning` interview row (detect first so the keep option is offered) and record the pick; never compute from the detection alone. With `custom` words you cannot compute from, stop and ask.
2. Next version, computed, never typed:
   - HeadVer: `yearweek` from `date -u +%g%V` on the build day; `head` from the newest HeadVer tag, or the profile's `versioning.head` for the first one. For a release that reaches users, offer "raise `head` to N+1 (recommended)" as a selection; never raise it silently, unless the project's rule says every release raises it. `build` must come from a counter that never repeats: a CI run number only where a re-run cannot repeat it. GitHub Actions keeps `run_number` on a re-run and raises only `run_attempt`, so there use `run_number` only when `run_attempt` is 1, or a dedicated counter.
   - SemVer: propose MAJOR, MINOR or PATCH from the change set since the newest release tag (`git log <tag>..HEAD`) and name the commits that drove it: any breaking change, MAJOR; else any feature, MINOR; else PATCH. A person decides. Under `0.y.z` anything may change (SemVer item 4), so a breaking change may raise only MINOR.
   - CalVer: from the release day and the profile's `format`, as the CalVer section says.
   - A release candidate takes the scheme's pre-release form below.
3. Unique: list the tags (`git tag --list`, and the remote's with `git ls-remote --tags origin`). The new tag must not exist yet, and a HeadVer build number names one build only; a release tag on the build of its `-dev` tag is the same build, not a reuse. Never reuse, move or delete a tag: a wrong published version is replaced by the next version.
4. Agree, per scheme:
   - HeadVer, or any version the pipeline stamps: the tag, the built artifact's `--version` and the release note show the same version. Never require or make a version-only commit to put it in a manifest.
   - SemVer or CalVer kept in the manifest: the manifest (`Cargo.toml`, `package.json`, `pyproject.toml`), the lock file, the changelog or release notes, install scripts and the built binary's `--version` agree with the tag.
5. Where: tag the merge commit on the integration branch after the merge, never on a feature branch.
6. Record: the version, its inputs (previous tag; build day and counter, the commits that drove the bump, or the day and format) and the tag name, in the release note and the card.
7. Refuse: when any step fails, do not tag or publish; report the step, its command and its output.

## SemVer

- Format: `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`, three non-negative integers without leading zeros. A tag usually carries a `v` in front (`v3.2.1`); the `v` is not part of the version.
- Who bumps what: a person decides from what the change does to users of the public interface. MAJOR for an incompatible change, MINOR for a feature added in a backward-compatible manner, PATCH for a backward-compatible fix. `0.y.z` is initial development, where anything may change.
- Pick it when: other code depends on the project's interface (a library, an API, a command line that scripts call) and a reader must know whether an upgrade can break them.
- Next version: from the newest release and the changes since it. Any incompatible change gives MAJOR plus one with MINOR and PATCH 0; else any feature gives MINOR plus one with PATCH 0; else PATCH plus one. The call is a human judgement; automation that reads commit messages (semantic-release style: `fix:` is PATCH, `feat:` is MINOR, a `BREAKING CHANGE` footer is MAJOR) makes it consistent, and only for a project that already writes such messages.
- Pre-release: a hyphen and dot-separated identifiers (`1.0.0-rc.1`), which sort before the release. Build metadata follows `+` (`1.0.0+build.7`) and is ignored in ordering.
- Examples: `v3.2.1` and a fix give `v3.2.2`; `v3.2.2` and an incompatible change give `v4.0.0`.

## HeadVer

- Format: `{head}.{yearweek}.{build}` (`3.2408.79`). Each number has one meaning:
  - `head`: set by a person, the only manual number, zero-based. The author recommends that you raise it on every delivery that reaches users; it then grows fast, and a large head (hundreds, past 1000) is fine.
  - `yearweek`: when it was built. Four digits: the two-digit year, then the two-digit ISO-8601 week (week 34 of 2021 is `2134`). Week rules differ between date libraries, so it is computed by a program at build time, never typed.
  - `build`: which build it is, from the build server's counter. Every build gets a new number and none is reused: two builds with one number mean the pipeline needs checking, and the same code built twice is two builds (a timestamp or packaging step can change the output).
- Who bumps what: a person raises `head`; the build pipeline stamps `yearweek` and `build`. Versions are tags the pipeline stamps on the build, never a version-file edit: never make a commit that only edits a version number, so the log holds only changes to behaviour (github.com/line/headver has a script per language).
- Metadata: text after `+` is not part of the version. It may be added freely; choose what helps communication, such as a platform (`3.2408.79+Android`); a git hash repeats what the build number says.
- Talk: plan by head (`v3`), debug by build (`b79`), and read the age from `yearweek`. Plan epics per head; a full version fixed before its build (`v4.xxxx.xx`) is usually a document template's demand, not a need. Rough 2024 seasons, in the article's labels: weeks 1–9 early year (to Mar 3), 10s spring (to May 12), 20s summer (to Jul 21), 30s autumn (to Sep 29), 40s winter (to Dec 8), 50–53 year end.
- Hotfix: raise `head` (it reaches users), or keep `head` and ship the fix as a new build. The article lists a third option, calling the hotfix by an alias such as `v22.1`; the author found it odd, because the alias exists nowhere in the system. MoAI's rule: never use such an alias.
- Pick it when: an end-user service product (an app or a web service) ships often from a build server, and people ask when it was built and which build it is. A public API or library fits SemVer better: there HeadVer raises the version for refactors that change nothing outside, which can confuse API users. A large, long-cycle product fits CalVer.
- Next version: keep `head` unless a person raised it; `yearweek` from the build day with `date -u +%g%V` (ISO week-year: 2025-12-29 gives `2601`; UTC is MoAI's choice, since the article names no time zone and the README's example uses the local `date +%V`); `build` from the build server's counter. Without such a counter the next version cannot be computed: say so and never invent the number.
- Pre-release: the scheme has no marking of its own. A hyphen suffix is allowed and advised against (raise `head` instead); a `+meta` suffix is acceptable. MoAI's rule: a HeadVer release candidate carries no suffix; it is a build tag marked as a pre-release on the release page (GitHub "Set as a pre-release"). Observed in the author's team screenshot (the article's image 07), not a rule: development builds tagged with a `-dev` suffix on the build counter shared with releases (`4.2408.39-dev`, `4.2408.39`, `4.2408.41-dev`, `4.2408.42`), and a release cut by picking a tag. A release tag on the same build number as its `-dev` tag names the same build, so it is not a reuse.
- Examples: `3.2408.79` is head 3, week 8 of 2024 (early year), build 79. `3.2647.59` is head 3, week 47 of 2026, build 59; the next build one week later is `3.2648.60`. A bug found in `5.2045.119` and fixed in `6.2047.124` was fixed in the next head, two weeks later. A project that moves from `v2.1.0` starts at head 3.

## CalVer

- Format: the project states its own, built from `YYYY` (full year), `YY` (short year: 6, 16, 106), `0Y` (zero-padded year), `M` and `0M` (month), `W` and `0W` (week of the year), `D` and `0D` (day), and `MAJOR`, `MINOR`, `MICRO`, `MODIFIER`. MoAI's default is `YYYY.0M.MICRO`. The profile takes two to four of these tokens joined by dots, at most one per unit, in the order year, month or week, day, `MINOR`, `MICRO`, `MODIFIER`, the year first; a day only behind a month (so never with a week), and `MODIFIER` not right behind the year (`MM`, `WW` and `DD`, the spelling before spec 26.0, are still read). `MAJOR` is refused: calver.org makes it the first number, and here the year is. So `YYYY.MINOR.MICRO` and `YYYY.0M.0D.MICRO` are taken, and `YYYY.0M.MM`, `YYYY.0D.0M`, `YYYY.0W.0D`, `YYYY.MODIFIER` and `YYYY.MAJOR` are not; any other form is recorded as `custom`.
- Who bumps what: the calendar sets the date segments on the release day. `MICRO` counts the releases inside one period from 0 and is the only number a person or a script increments.
- Pick it when: time or outside change drives the releases more than interface changes do (a large or constantly changing scope, a time-sensitive project). It does not signal a breaking change: pair it with a changelog and a deprecation policy.
- Next version: take the date segments from the release day. When they equal the newest release's, `MICRO` plus one; otherwise `MICRO` 0. A format that ends in the day (`YYYY.0M.0D`) appends a micro segment for a second release on the same day.
- Pre-release: the `MODIFIER`, written the way the project's tags already write it; with nothing to follow, `-rc.1` (`2026.11.0-rc.1`).
- Examples: with `YYYY.0M.MICRO`, `2026.11.0` and a second release in November give `2026.11.1`, and the first one in December is `2026.12.0`. Elsewhere: Ubuntu `YY.0M` (`26.04`), yt-dlp `YYYY.0M.0D` (`2026.03.17`), pip `YY.MINOR.MICRO`.
